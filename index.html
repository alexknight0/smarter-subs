Learn Korean from the shows you already watch

Smarter Subtitles is a Chrome extension that shows Korean and English Netflix subtitles at the same time, highlights the words you don't know yet, and hides the English once you can read the sentence yourself.

Coming soon to the Chrome Web Store

What it does

Two subtitles, in step

Korean and English appear and leave together, even when Netflix's two tracks are timed a little differently. You choose which language goes on top and how big each one is.

Jump line by line

Press A to hear the last line again and D to skip to the next one. The new line shows straight away, and playback starts just before it so you hear the first syllable. On-screen buttons do the same with the mouse.

Hover for the dictionary

Hovering a Korean word pauses the show and opens its entry from the National Institute of Korean Language's learners' dictionary, with the dictionary form (했어 → 하다) and grammar patterns such as -ㄴ 적이 있다.

Your vocabulary, word by word

Words you don't know yet are yellow. Right-click one for learning, again for known. Names are blue and never counted. Your marks follow you into every episode.

English only when you need it

When you know every word in a sentence, its English blurs or disappears so you read the Korean first. Press E or hover to check yourself. You can allow a few unknown words per sentence.

A head start

Start with a word list instead of marking 하다 and 사람 by hand: the NIKL vocabulary grades A to C, TOPIK beginner or intermediate words, or the most common words in Korean Netflix subtitles.

Sentences, not lines

Netflix often splits a sentence over two subtitles. Smarter Subtitles knows when a line carries on, shows "1/2" beside it, and judges the whole sentence when deciding whether you need the English.

Other languages too

When a character speaks English, Japanese or any language other than Korean, the English goes on top and is never hidden. This uses Netflix's forced subtitles, which some titles have.

Ask AI and fix the analysis

Stuck on a phrase? Ask AI explains the word in its sentence, using your own Gemini key. If a word was split wrongly, Fix lets you split or join it, and the fix applies everywhere.

Both languages at once

Netflix only shows one subtitle track at a time. Smarter Subtitles shows the Korean and the English together, so you can hear the line, read it in Korean and check what it means without pausing.

Lines that belong to a longer sentence are marked "1/2" and "2/2".

A dictionary that reads the sentence

Korean words often have several unrelated meanings. The popup puts the meaning that fits the part of speech first, highlights the one that matches the English subtitle, and shows grammar patterns the word is part of.

Still unsure? Ask AI explains what the word is doing in that sentence. Right-click the word to mark it learning or known without leaving the popup.

Open the full entry in one click, click the word to pin the popup, or press Esc to close it and carry on watching.

The English steps back as you learn

While a sentence still has words you don't know, the English goes on top so you can follow the story. Once you know them all, only the Korean is left. You choose whether the English blurs or disappears, and E brings it back.

The more words you mark, the more of each episode you read in Korean.

Review sentences in the scene they came from

When you watch a sentence whose words you know or are learning, it is saved. Each day the Review page suggests some of them, spread across your learning words, and you add the ones you want to review.

Start review replays each sentence from its episode, with the lines around it, then asks what it means. Reveal the English and answer Understood? No or Yes. Sentences come back on Anki's schedule: in minutes at first, then tomorrow, then at longer and longer intervals.

Reviews play on a separate Netflix profile of your choice, so your place in each show and your Continue Watching row stay as they were. When the review ends you are back where you started.

Words graduate on their own

A learning word becomes known once several of its sentences are on long review intervals. Promotions on the Review page lists these words, and the words you marked known yourself, so you can approve or deny each one before it joins the list of words you know.

A remote for the couch

Casting the Chrome tab to your TV? Open the remote page on your phone and sign in with the same Google account.

Your phone shows the subtitles in time with the show. Tap a word for its dictionary entry and mark it known, unknown or learning. Big buttons play, pause, jump between lines and skip 10 seconds, and you can change the volume from there too.

Everywhere you watch

Sign in with Google and your marked words, word fixes, review sentences and settings sync between computers. Without signing in, everything stays on this computer.

Word analysis runs inside the extension with the Kiwi Korean morphological analyser, so subtitles aren't sent anywhere to be analysed.

Questions

What do I need?

Chrome (or another Chromium browser) on a desktop or laptop, and a Netflix subscription. Browser extensions can't run inside the Netflix apps on phones and TVs. To watch on a TV, cast the Chrome tab or plug the laptop in over HDMI and use the phone remote.

Does it work with every show?

It works with any title that has both Korean and English subtitles in your region. Netflix doesn't offer every subtitle language for every show.

Is it only for Korean?

The dictionary, word marking and reviews are built for Korean learners who read English. Showing two subtitle languages at once works for other language pairs too.

Does it change my Netflix account?

No. It adds the subtitles over Netflix's player in your browser. Reviews use a separate Netflix profile that you pick, so your watch progress isn't moved.

How much does it cost?

The extension is free. Ask AI is optional and uses your own Gemini API key.
